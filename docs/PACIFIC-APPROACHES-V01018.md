## Result and scope

The extended-approach mechanism now applies at five selected Pacific anchors, the North Sea and west of Ireland, in addition to Gibraltar, Sicily and the Channel. Each approach reads a wider stretch of its route rather than only the first shared leg. Its signed bearing varies the exit distance while retaining the shared hub tangent and a matching tangent on the retained path. Both ends reserve space against the same original route length, preventing processing-order differences. Candidates contract when the water mask rejects them or a long existing corner would require overshooting the intended exit. No new shared junction is manufactured at an unrelated crossing.

69 shipping routes and 68 cable routes now have at least one extended approach; these are totals including the earlier European treatment, not added connections. Pacific endpoint coverage at default settings: Japan 15 ship/16 cable approaches, Hawaii 9/10, northern California 6/8, southern California 6/7, Guam 0/4. Guam's two shipping routes have different bearing families and are not forced into a bundle. Selected reaches span approximately 0.24–5.06 degrees of illustrative route arc. Straight and unbundled settings bypass the extensions.

Four additional European display spines refine the Rotterdam–Ireland, western Scottish and Skagerrak approaches. The Rotterdam feeder now passes Dover instead of circling Scotland; the western Scottish route still uses the northern passage. Two Pacific spines provide a gradual Guam–Japan shoulder and the California offshore connection. Total European spines: 37. Source records, all endpoints, source counts (502/410), moving populations and distribution settings remain intact.

As the camera approaches, secondary marine strand exposure receives a smooth gain of up to 32%, gated by existing crowding. Dense knots keep the previous budget; zero secondary-light settings still produce zero secondary light. Moving local guides return before this adjustment, and flights are excluded. No added colours, sliders or timing changes.

## Limits and preservation

Coarse relief and authored offshore anchors still limit coastal precision. Some geometric bows and distinct crossings remain intentional or schematic; this is not surveyed cable alignment, measured shipping traffic or global junction completion. Captures and sampled geometry do not establish physical-device, sustained-performance or subjective acceptance.
